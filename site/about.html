About Cammelot

Independent research into the systemic boundaries of AI in public infrastructure.

Cammelot is an independent educational simulation exploring assumptions about AI, care access and public infrastructure. It uses selected Dutch statistics as context, but its outputs describe authored models, not measured effects of technology on Dutch healthcare.

The Scope of the Project

The current Minister game follows 45 fictional named residents in a town-scale setting of 5,000, with two modeled GP practices and a hospital. These are synthetic profiles, not real patient records. The minister role, powers, scenario budget and intervention prices are authored abstractions, not a literal national financing system. CBS/RIVM context and selected tariffs do not validate disease transitions, policy coefficients, political rules or mortality attribution.

Evidence classes: distinguish dated empirical input data, model assumptions, authored game rules and observed simulation outputs. A model-classified system death is not a clinically established preventable death. The public evidence contract and source checklist explain these limits.

Technical Architecture and Lineage

Park et al.'s 2023 Generative Agents work informed the design vocabulary. The modular backend contains FHIR/A2A and cognitive-loop experiments; the legacy World page and current Minister page are separate browser executables. They do not become clinically validated by sharing that architecture or vocabulary.

The archived 200-run study used the World executable through scripts/research_run.cjs. Its June 2026 artifact records run count and cycles but not the exact executable revision. Reported legacy figures therefore have unverified provenance at the individual-claim level and do not validate Minister. Current Minister comparisons must identify their own model version, executable, seed, ending tick and command history.

About the Author

I am Simone Cammel, and I have worked in Big Tech for the last six years. Because of my professional background in the technology sector, it should be assumed that I have a bias toward the potential of artificial intelligence. This is the reason I built Cammelot as a “falsification machine” to see if a data-driven model could prove those biases wrong.

I believe that the current discussion regarding AI in healthcare is often focused on the wrong objectives. Many see AI as a way to increase the speed of existing tasks, such as writing emails or taking notes; however, I believe the real potential lies in restructuring how entire systems operate. This simulation is an attempt to be honest about which problems AI can solve and which ones it might worsen.

Open Source Commitment

Transparency is a core requirement for this project, and therefore all code and simulation data are available for public review. If you find a flaw in the logic or a mistake in the statistical analysis, I encourage you to share your findings. The project is designed to be a collaborative effort where the parameters can be adjusted by anyone who wishes to test a different hypothesis.

Primary Data Sources

The following sources provide empirical or policy context. They do not establish every model parameter or validate the resulting simulation outcomes:

  • CBS, Centraal Bureau voor de Statistiek - population demographics and baseline mortality tables
  • RIVM, Rijksinstituut voor Volksgezondheid en Milieu - age-stratified disease prevalence and comorbidity risk factors
  • NZa, Nederlandse Zorgautoriteit - healthcare tariffs and access-standard oversight (Treeknorm); not a universal 12-week statutory limit
  • LHV, Landelijke Huisartsen Vereniging - administrative workload and capacity of general practitioners
  • IZA, Integraal Zorgakkoord - transformation targets for system interoperability and staffing
